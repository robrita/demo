## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the bank's requirements for protecting personal and sensitive personal information of customers, employees, and third parties in compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) and BSP regulations.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all employees, contractors, consultants, and third-party service providers who access, process, store, or transmit personal data.

## 3. Data Classification

| Classification | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Information approved for public release | Published interest rates, branch locations |
| Internal | For bank employee use only | Internal memos, org charts, SOP documents |
| Confidential | Customer or business-sensitive data | Account balances, transaction history, credit scores |
| Restricted | Highly sensitive, regulatory-protected data | Passwords, PINs, biometric data, government IDs, medical records |

## 4. Handling Requirements

### 4.1 Data Collection

- Collect only the minimum data necessary for the stated purpose.
- Inform the customer of the purpose, scope, and retention period via the Data Privacy Notice (DPN-01).
- Obtain documented consent before processing sensitive personal information.

### 4.2 Data Storage

- Confidential and Restricted data must be encrypted at rest (AES-256 minimum).
- Paper documents containing personal data must be stored in locked cabinets with access logs.
- Data retention follows the Records Retention Schedule (POL-2024-010).

### 4.3 Data Access

- Access is granted on a need-to-know basis per the user's role.
- Access to Restricted data requires manager approval and is logged.
- Shared credentials are prohibited.

### 4.4 Data Sharing

- Customer data may only be shared with third parties under a signed Data Sharing Agreement (DSA).
- Cross-border data transfers require DPO approval and must comply with NPC guidelines.
- Customer data must not be shared via personal email, messaging apps, or unapproved cloud services.

### 4.5 Authorized Representatives and Medical Emergency

- Customer information may only be released to a third party upon presentation of:
  - (a) A notarized Special Power of Attorney (SPA) granted by the customer, OR
  - (b) A court order or court-appointed guardianship, OR
  - (c) A written authorization from the customer's attending physician, limited to essential account actions only (e.g., pension withdrawal, bill payment), not full account disclosure.
- In all cases, the representative must present their own valid government-issued ID and the original authorization document.
- Photocopies of the SPA, court order, or medical authorization must be retained in the customer's file.
- If the customer is incapacitated and no prior authorization exists, the requesting party must be advised to obtain a court-appointed guardianship or legal representation.
- The Branch Manager must approve any release of information to a third party under this section.
- The Data Protection Office must be notified for any sensitive data disclosure under medical emergency provisions.
- Verbal or telephone requests for third-party access are NOT permitted regardless of the claimed relationship to the customer.

### 4.6 Data Disposal

- Electronic data must be securely deleted using approved tools.
- Paper documents must be shredded using cross-cut shredders.
- Disposal must be logged in the Data Disposal Register.

## 5. Incident Response

### 5.1 Data Breach Reporting

1. Any employee who discovers or suspects a data breach must report it to the Data Protection Office within 1 hour.
2. The DPO assesses the breach and determines if NPC notification is required (within 72 hours for notifiable breaches).
3. Affected customers are notified if the breach is likely to cause harm.

### 5.2 Breach Categories

| Category | Description | Notification Required |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | Misdirected email, no sensitive data exposed | Internal only |
| Moderate | Unauthorized access to Confidential data | DPO assessment |
| Major | Large-scale exposure of Restricted data | NPC + customer notification |

## 6. Employee Responsibilities

- Complete annual data privacy training.
- Lock workstations when unattended (clean desk policy).
- Report suspicious emails, phishing attempts, and unauthorized access.
- Do not discuss customer information in public areas.

## 7. Penalties

- Violations may result in disciplinary action up to termination.
- Violations of RA 10173 may carry criminal penalties including imprisonment and fines.
